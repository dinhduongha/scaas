One agent, one pass, on branch `governance` at `190fd702`. Targets: [permission-model.md](permission-model.md) (v2), [permission-model-gaps.md](permission-model-gaps.md),
[prototype/RESULTS.md](prototype/RESULTS.md) with `GovernanceBase`, `GovernanceLib`, `GovGrant.t.sol`, and CTR-GOV-01 to -10. A1-A21 of [permission-model-attack.md](permission-model-attack.md) are not

This is a review by one AI reader. It is not an audit, and everything cryptographic here stays `UNAUDITED`.

Severity: **P0** funds or a binding rule broken as written, exploitable; **P1** must change before the spec or before a freeze; **P2** a real hole, bounded, or reached through a privileged mistake; **P3** wording, robustness, a
figure. Proof: `test/governance/GovAttackV2.t.sol`. Each `test_attack_*` passes while its hole is open and holds a positive control, so a fix turns it red; each `test_refuted_*` is an attack that failed.

## 1. Verdict

**No P0.** No path found lets a stranger take a power, and a grantee cannot widen its own terms. The core of `GovernanceLib` (two levels, `narrows`, epoch kill, spent carried on a same-terms edit, the renounce rule on its own path) held
against every direct attempt. What does not hold is what surrounds it: the delay stops at `grantRole`, the holder note copies the two faults A1 and A2 fixed on the token, a reserved enum id is not a value, the scope words of the owner's own examples bind only at
grant, and three statements of "holds everywhere" (the renounce rule, "an edit never refreshes", "one rule on every chain") are true of one path each.

Five P1, 17 P2, 12 P3.
